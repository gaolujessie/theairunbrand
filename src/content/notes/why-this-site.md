For ten years I have run a children's clothing brand the way most founders run a brand: by doing nearly everything myself, and hiring when I couldn't. Design, sampling, production briefs, wholesale accounts, the website, the marketing, the customer emails at eleven at night. The brand grew. It is sold at Harrods and John Lewis, across ten e-commerce platforms, and to customers worldwide. The list of jobs never got shorter.

Two years ago I started moving those jobs, one at a time, onto AI — mainly Claude. Not as an experiment on the side, but inside the real business, on real product, with real customers who would notice if the quality slipped. Some of it worked immediately. Some of it took months to get right. Some of it I have switched off.

This site is where I write that down.

## What these notes are

Each note is an operating record, not an opinion piece. The shape is always the same:

- **The job** — a real task in the business, and roughly how much time or money it used to take.
- **The old way** — how it was actually done before, including the parts that were quietly broken.
- **The AI way** — the setup that replaced it, in enough detail to copy: tools, workflow, what the agent is and isn't allowed to do.
- **What it cost** — in money, in my time, and in the mistakes along the way.
- **What broke** — because something always did.
- **What you can copy** — the part of it that would transfer to a different brand in a different category.

The first three will be case studies: AI photography against a real shoot, ten outfits side by side; building an AI team for customer service and marketing, and why the Instagram caption workflow was the hardest part; and how a London brand with a China warehouse and customers in Tokyo actually runs day to day, and exactly where AI now sits in it.

## Who this is for

Founders of consumer brands, mostly. People who make a physical product and sell it to real people, who are doing most of the work themselves, and who have been told that AI will change everything without being shown a single workflow that does. If you are at the point where the next step looks like hiring five people you can't yet afford, these notes are for you.

If you run a bigger brand and want to know what one person plus agents can genuinely cover before you make your next hire, they are for you too.

## What I'm not going to do

I am not going to publish my brand's accounts, and I am not going to pretend the AI did things it didn't. Where I give numbers they will be relative — a shoot that took three weeks now takes three days — because those are the numbers that help you decide, and they keep my business private. Where something failed, I will say so.

One note a week. If that is useful to you, there is an email signup on the home page.

— Jessie
